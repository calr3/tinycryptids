# A Turing machine for Gilbreath's conjecture

**Current best: `machines/gilbreath_97.txt`, a 97-state, 2-symbol Turing machine**
(named-state format, see `../tools/tm.py`). Started on a blank tape, it **halts iff
Gilbreath's conjecture fails**: iff for some `k ≥ 1` the first entry of row `k` of the
triangle of absolute differences of the primes `2, 3, 5, 7, …` is not 1.
Lean proves this (`TM.Gilbreath.M97.halts_iff`):

    Halts M97.delta2 M97.start2 ↔
      ∃ N k, 1 ≤ k ∧ k < (primesBelow N).length ∧ (gilbreathRow (primesBelow N) k).head? ≠ some 1

`primesBelow`, `absDiffs` and `gilbreathRow` are in `../lean/TinyCryptids/Defs.lean`.
`RECORDS.md` lists the machines, and `PROOF.md` walks through the proof in plain words.

The 97-state machine is obtained in two stages:

1. `gilbreath.txt` is the readable **151-state design**, generated by `build.py`. The Lean proof
   is written for this machine (`TM.Gilbreath.halts_iff`).
2. `../tools/minimize.py` merges states of the design that never disagree on a transition the
   run uses (an exact SAT search; `cadical195` and `kissat404`). `gen_reduced_lean.py` carries
   the theorem over, and `machines/gilbreath_97_annotated.txt` is the same machine with each state
   named after its roles in the design.

## The search

The last diagonal of the triangle on the primes found so far is `D0, D1, …, D(n-1)`, with `D0`
the last prime `p` (the last entries of rows 0, 1, …). A new prime `m` gives the new diagonal

    m, |m - D0|, ||m - D0| - D1|, …

and its last entry is the single entry, hence the first entry, of the new bottom row: it must be 1.
`D0` is not stored. With `g = m - p` the stored list `S = D1, …, D(n-1)` becomes
`g, |g - D1|, ||g - D1| - D2|, …`, whose last entry is the one to check.

    S := [1], g := 1, m := 4            (after the primes 2, 3)
    loop:
        if m is prime:  S := [g, |g - S1|, ||g - S1| - S2|, …];  if last S ≠ 1: HALT;  g := 0
        g := g + 1, m := m + 1

## How it works

All numbers are in unary. The tape, left to right (separators `00` or `000`; a hole is one 0):

    G 00 M 00 S(n-1) 000 … 000 S1 000 [zeros] 00 D 00 R

* **G** = `1 0 1^(g+1)`: the gap since the last prime, with a hole at cell 1. `G += 1` moves the
  hole one cell left and adds a cell on the left.
* **M**, one cell, marks the start of the stored list; `S(i)` has `S(i) + 2` cells.
* **D 00 R** tests the candidate `m = d + r` by trial division, exactly as in `../legendre`: D has
  `d + 2` cells with a hole at cell `c + 1` (a residue counter), R has `r + 2` cells and a hole
  walking through it. If `d | m`, m is composite; else `d += 1`, `r -= 1`; at `r = 0` m is prime.
  Afterwards `D := 2`, `R := m - 1` for the next candidate.

**A composite m**: G += 1, then back to D for the rebuild.

**A prime m: the update.** The list is rebuilt as a queue: the old values are taken from its right
end (the block **B** next to the zeros), the new ones are made at the left end of the tape. **L**
is the newest value, first G; **Z** is made two cells left of it as `1 0 1` (value 0), with a hole
at cell 1 like G. One step, `Z := |L - B|`:

1. L's hole is a cursor: it advances one cell at a time, `L` times. After each advance the machine
   walks right to B (across holes and separators, until four 0s), and either erases B's last cell
   (`B -= 1`) or, if B is down to 0, adds a cell to Z (`Z += 1`: Z's hole moves left and a cell is
   added).
2. L exhausted: while `B > 0`, `B -= 1` and `Z += 1`.
3. B (now 0) is erased, and L loses its hole and its last cell (it becomes a stored block). Z is the
   next L.

When the next block left of the erased B is M, the update is over: M is erased, L becomes a stored
block, and Z, the new last entry, must be `1 0 1 1` (value 1), else the machine HALTS. Z becomes a
stored block, and a new M and `G = 1 0 1 1` are written left of it.

**Seed.** The seed writes the tape after the primes 2 and 3 (`S = [1]`, `g = 1`) and the D/R region
of `m = 3` in the middle of its rebuild, so the run starts with the candidate 4.
